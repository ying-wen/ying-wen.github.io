# Research Source and Scope Map

> Source snapshot: 24 August 2026
> Scope: sources used by the Chinese Blog Edition and position paper. This map distinguishes observed results, developer reports, theoretical proposals, position papers, interviews, secondary reporting, and investor scenarios.

## How to read this map

- **Observed / peer-reviewed**: the cited work reports empirical or theoretical results through an academic venue. The result may still be narrow.
- **Preprint**: primary research report without relying on venue acceptance as an authority signal.
- **First-party report**: the developer reports its own system results or deployment. Useful, but not independent replication.
- **Position / program**: a definition, hypothesis, or research agenda rather than a settled result.
- **Interview**: attributed statements and research judgments from named speakers; not a substitute for the underlying paper.
- **Secondary reporting**: a journalistic interpretation used to locate public framing; not technical validation.
- **Investor / scenario**: material about incentives, capital, or possible futures; not a scientific validation of capability mechanisms.

## Claims introduced by this paper

The following are synthesis or proposals, not facts attributed to a single source:

1. Search Scaling as the family \(\Gamma_h=\{(c,J_{\mathrm{task}}(c\mid h,z,D)):c>0\}\), with persistent state and the search program \(h=(\phi_h,\mathcal A_h,q_h,v_h,\mu_h,\sigma_h)\) fixed; design-level search requires a separate outer controller over \(h\);
2. the operational definitions separating learning, training, testing, deployment, inference, reasoning, search, planning, adaptation, harness, task-local state, and persistent state;
3. the distinction between training scaling, experience scaling, Learning Scaling as the multi-output response \(\mathcal L(n)\) over continuing control, recovery, transfer, retention, late plasticity, and fresh-task skill-acquisition efficiency, and recursive Learning Scaling in which \(n_{\rho,t}\) falls and \(\eta_t(\rho)\) rises across stages;
4. Search and Learning as mechanisms, Self-evolving as loop autonomy, AI4AI as application scope, and RSI as feedback dynamics under declared capability metrics and fixed exogenous inputs;
5. the use of total feedback elasticity \(E_{\dot I,I\mid X}>0\) for the paper's positive-feedback RSI criterion, separated from the \(>1\) self-maintaining-acceleration condition in the simplest stock–flow model;
6. the two RSI routes through AI-R&amp;D search productivity and lifetime learning, with fresh-task skill-acquisition efficiency treated as an intermediate edge rather than sufficient evidence of RSI;
7. the separation between logged-experience replay, which isolates the update operator, and paired continuing lifetimes, which preserve policy-induced experience; the response family \(J_{run},T_{recover},\mathrm{Reg}_{dyn},J_{new},n_{\rho,t},R_{old},P_{late}\); and the two-factor decomposition of Search, Learning, and their interaction \(\Delta_I\);
8. the four falsifiable position claims and their corresponding controlled comparisons;
9. the ordered expansion from output candidates to reasoning trajectories, action trajectories, harness design, and AI-design search, together with the separate persistent-learning path;
10. the distinction between a committed successor from design search, iterated cross-version design search, and lifetime development of one continuing identity;
11. the separation of the externally defined terminal-behavior set \(\Omega(x)\) from the harness-constructed search graph \(G_h(x,z)\), reachable support \(\mathcal U_h(x,z)\), and finite-compute occupancy distribution \(\rho_{h,c}\), together with the decomposition into representation/reachability and visitation/evaluation/selection gaps;
12. the distinction between a local high-versus-low contrast and a scaling relation measured over multiple \(c\) or \(n\) levels, without assuming a power-law form;
13. the distinction between harness proposal quality in an outer loop and harness utilization by the object-level task policy;
14. the finite-horizon total-cost condition \(C_U+\sum_iC_i^+<\sum_iC_i^-\), together with the requirement that every comparison uses one declared unit and remains separate from the Learning Scaling claim;
15. compute-conditioned harness selection: \(h^\star(c_0;z,D)\) for fixed inner-task compute and \(h^\star(P_C;z,D)\) for a task-dependent inner-compute distribution, together with the separation of outer design-search compute from inner task-execution compute.
16. the narrower interpretation of the synthetic-data dispute: synthetic experience can improve a search prior, but shared omissions in the generator, reward, and evaluator are not corrected merely by generating more samples; deployment feedback matters when it can persistently revise future decisions;
17. the autonomous-driving decomposition of real logged data, simulation, pretraining, RL post-training, guided search, offline model replacement, and runtime continual learning; the decomposition is introduced by this paper and is not attributed as a completed system to any company in the interview.

These proposals should be judged by whether they produce cleaner experiments and falsifiable predictions, not by whether the terminology becomes popular.
